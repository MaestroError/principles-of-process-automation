# Notes from a call — "the thing I do every Monday"

Transcribed from a working session. The speaker is a customer success lead
describing a task she repeats weekly and wants to hand off.

---

So every Monday morning I pull the list of accounts that renewed or churned in
the last seven days. That comes out of our CRM as a CSV — I export it myself,
there's a saved view called "Weekly renewal movement". It's got account name,
ARR before, ARR after, renewal date, owner, and a status field that's one of
renewed, churned, downgraded, or upgraded.

Then I go through and I work out which ones actually need talking about at the
Monday sync. Not all of them do. If it renewed flat and it's under about 20k
ARR, nobody cares, that's just the machine working. What people care about is:
anything that churned at all, anything that downgraded by more than about 15%,
anything over 100k ARR whatever happened to it, and anything where the owner
changed in the last quarter, because that's usually a sign the handover went
badly.

Oh — and if an account churned but it's a subsidiary of a parent that's still
with us, that's a completely different conversation and I flag it separately.
That one catches people out. It looks like churn in the export and it isn't,
really.

Then for each one that made the cut I write two or three lines. What happened,
what the number was, and what I think the reason is. The reason bit I have to
get from the notes field or from Slack, the export doesn't have it. Sometimes I
just can't find it and I say "reason unknown" — I used to guess and that went
badly once, someone repeated my guess to the customer.

Then it all goes into a doc in a particular shape. Headline number at the top —
net ARR movement for the week. Then churn, then downgrades, then the big
renewals, then anything else notable. Each section is bullets. I keep the whole
thing under a page because nobody reads past that.

Things that go wrong: sometimes the export is stale, the saved view didn't
refresh and I get last week's data. I've learned to check the max renewal date
against today before I start. Sometimes an account appears twice because it has
two contracts, and I have to merge those or the ARR numbers double-count.

It takes me about ninety minutes and it's the same ninety minutes every week.

If someone asks me to do it for a month instead of a week the shape's basically
the same but the thresholds should move, otherwise you get forty bullets.
